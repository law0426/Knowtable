Working premise — this is still more of a pile of decisions than a synopsis.

A fire damaged the West Wing of Bellwether's municipal archive a little over two years ago. The official report settled on an electrical fault, although the first report was more cautious. A bound transfer register survived, but one page is missing. The missing page may be boring: a list of records moved out of the building before the fire. That would actually be worse than a confession, because it would mean someone had time to make the paperwork look ordinary.

Mara Venn used to work in the archive. She left after refusing to sign a revised inventory. She's back in the building because a retired clerk has told her the register was never destroyed, as she'd been led to believe.

Tomas, her brother, works for municipal facilities and knew the building's service routes. He says he didn't enter the West Wing that night. The access log says his badge was used at the service entrance, but the log itself has been amended once, so it isn't a clean smoking gun.

I don't want this to turn into a puzzle where the cleverest person spots one clue and wins. The records should establish what can be known, not hand Mara a complete answer. Her problem is partly that she can prove somebody altered the record, but not whether exposing it will hurt the people who were trying to keep the archive's contents from disappearing altogether.

Loose questions:
- Why was the revised inventory treated as routine when it changed the status of several boxes?
- Who had authority to move original documents during the recovery?
- If Tomas did go inside, what was he trying to retrieve? Don't make the answer automatically sinister.
- The ending should make the truth clearer but the decision harder.
