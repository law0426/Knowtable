Usually answers a narrower question than the one asked. It looks like evasiveness, but most of the time she's trying not to claim more than she can prove.

When nervous, gets very exact about trivial details: the time on a receipt, the order of the labels, whether a door was pulled shut or allowed to swing. She can tell you what is missing from a file faster than she can explain why the missing part matters.

Doesn't like confident explanations. An admitted uncertainty is easier for her to work with than a polished story with no gaps in it. Unfortunately, she's started treating silence as a deliberate choice. If somebody doesn't answer, she assumes they're withholding something; she doesn't always consider that they may be embarrassed, tired, or genuinely unsure.

Keeps small damaged scraps of paper in an envelope. The habit started after she had to reconstruct a register from fragments during a records move. It became a way of keeping track of details that might otherwise be dismissed as insignificant. She'd deny that the habit comforts her.

Under pressure she becomes more controlled, not more forceful. The sharper her questions get, the less likely she is to say what she's actually afraid of.
