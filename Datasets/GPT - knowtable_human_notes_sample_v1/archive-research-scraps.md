Research to verify before the archive details get any more specific:

- What would a municipal archive actually record when boxes are moved temporarily during emergency recovery? I expect a movement log or transfer sheet, but need a plausible version for this setting, not a modern ideal process.
- Who signs off on an item being marked destroyed? Archivist? Facilities? Insurer's assessor? Probably not the same person in every case.
- Fire damage isn't one thing. Heat, smoke, water from firefighting and later mould can affect documents differently. Need a believable reason why one register is legible enough to matter while other material is not.
- Could a page be removed without making the stitching obviously disturbed? Ask a conservator, or at minimum find a credible preservation source. Don't overstate what Mara can tell by looking.
- How long are access-card logs retained in a city building? Who can amend them, and is there an audit trail for that amendment?
- The point isn't that procedure always works. The interesting gap is between the procedure written down and the shortcuts people take when a building is damaged and everybody wants it reopened.

Useful language to look up later: accession record, transfer register, condition report, chain of custody, conservation assessment, access log. These are candidate terms, not yet facts in the manuscript.
