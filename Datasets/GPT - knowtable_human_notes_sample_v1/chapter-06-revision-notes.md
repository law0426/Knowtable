This scene is doing too many things at once. Keep the discovery small. The missing page should change Mara's understanding, not solve the fire.

Tomas's line 'It was supposed to be' is probably too neat. He wouldn't hand her a useful ambiguity just because the scene wants one. Maybe he says the register was on the destruction list and then hears how that sounds. Or he genuinely thinks the whole volume was meant to be destroyed because of water damage.

Need a physical reason for him to be in the cellar. He could be checking a leak, but that feels like author machinery unless the setting establishes the pipes earlier. Alternative: he knows the door has been left unsecured and comes to shut it, not knowing she's inside.

Don't let Mara immediately infer that Tomas cut the page. She knows the page was removed; she doesn't know when, by whom, or whether it happened before the fire. Keep those as separate questions.

The envelope on the table: too much of a visual cue? It exists in her character notes, but a reader hasn't been shown it yet. Either introduce it earlier or take it out of this beat.

Need tension, but not everyone talking in riddles. Let one of them misunderstand the other for an ordinary reason.
