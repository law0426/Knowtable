Tomas Venn — municipal facilities electrician, now supervising maintenance contractors. He knows which service doors stick, which alarm sensors have failed before, and how much of the old building was altered without the plans being updated.

He is practical in a way that can read as indifference. If something is broken, he wants to know what it will take to make it work again. He has very little patience for a process that produces three reports and no repair. This is partly sensible and partly how he avoids talking about things he can't fix.

On the night of the fire, he says he was called about a fault in another part of the building and never entered the West Wing. A badge assigned to him appears in the service-entry log shortly before the alarm. He says the badge was in his locker. The contractor who had temporary access that week hasn't been interviewed yet, or at least Mara hasn't found a record of it.

He and his sister still speak, mostly about their mother and ordinary logistics. They can spend ten minutes arguing about whether a hinge needs replacing and avoid the one question they both came to ask.

Don't make him secretly evil. He may have moved something, or let someone use his access, or be covering for a mistake that turned into something much worse. He needs a reason to be afraid of what the records will show, not just a convenient guilty face.
