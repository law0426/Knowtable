The building was extended in stages, so the floor plan tells several different stories at once. The public counter and council chambers feel orderly; the old West Wing is full of awkward joints where new corridors meet older rooms. There are signs explaining the official route, but staff use shorter routes through service doors and stores.

The basement isn't a dramatic dungeon. Low pipes, patched paint, a trolley that never quite rolls straight. The archive's temporary recovery area is in a room that used to hold maintenance supplies. It has a sink, a metal table and a door that closes by itself unless someone wedges it open. The door's latch can be heard from the far end of the corridor.

The building is quiet in uneven ways: the ventilation hum continues after the lights go out; water knocks in the pipes; sound travels up the stairwell but dies behind the fire doors. Avoid making every sound ominous. Most of this is ordinary, and that makes the few unexplained noises more noticeable.

For scene blocking: if Mara is standing at the table, someone at the cellar door can see the open register but probably not read the cut edge. Tomas should have to take a few steps into the room before he can tell what's on the page. The service entrance and the cellar are connected, but not directly; there's a short corridor and one turn between them.

Question: was the basement recovery room actually locked after the fire, or was it only marked staff-only? The answer affects how suspicious it is that either of them can get in.
