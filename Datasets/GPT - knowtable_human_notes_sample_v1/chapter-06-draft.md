The register was smaller than she'd expected. Mara turned it sideways beneath the lamp and tried not to breathe through her mouth.

Most of the writing had run. Names dissolved into blue-grey streaks, dates into little branches of ink. The page she'd come for was almost untouched, except that it wasn't there. The binding showed a narrow ridge where the paper had been cut close to the stitching.

She checked the cover, then the table, then the floor around the table. It was possible the page had been removed before the fire. It was possible she'd remembered the number wrong. Both explanations were possible; neither made her feel better.

Behind her, the cellar door clicked shut.

'You're not supposed to be down here,' Tomas said.

She kept looking at the binding. 'Neither were you, apparently.'

For a second, he didn't say anything. The light hummed above them. Somewhere in the pipework, water knocked once, then again.

'Who told you the register was here?'

'You first.'

He looked at the open book, and then at the envelope beside her hand. The expression didn't last long enough to name.

Mara closed the register. Dust had settled on the cover except where her palm had pushed it away. 'I thought this had been destroyed.'

'It was supposed to be.'

She had a question ready, several in fact, but that answer rearranged them. She couldn't tell whether he'd admitted to moving the register, or merely repeated what somebody else had told him.
